For parents and families

What the council does, the standards every chapter must meet, and who to call with a question or concern.

Our mission

The Independent Interfraternity Council brings UNR's fraternities together under one shared set of standards, and we hold ourselves to them.

We exist to keep members safe, help them succeed in the classroom, serve the Reno community, and make sure every chapter lives up to the values it was founded on. When a chapter falls short, our own judicial board holds it accountable.

Our standards

Every chapter agrees to these as a condition of membership in the council. They come straight from our constitution and bylaws.

No hazing
Hazing is prohibited in every part of chapter life, formal or informal.
Alcohol-free recruitment
Recruitment events and new member education are free of alcohol and drugs.
A short new member period
New member education lasts no longer than twelve weeks.
Academics come first
Each chapter must keep a cumulative GPA of at least 2.70.
Written safety policies
Each chapter follows risk management policies on alcohol and drugs, hazing, sexual abuse and harassment, and fire, health, and safety.
Freedom to leave
A student can turn down a bid, or leave the new member process at any time, without penalty or pressure.

What it costs

Council dues are $15 per member each semester, paid through the chapter.

Each chapter sets its own dues on top of that, and they vary. Ask the chapter's president for a full breakdown before your student accepts a bid.

Who to contact

Questions about the council or fraternity life in general go to the council president. For questions about a specific chapter, reach out to that chapter.

Chapter presidents

    Report a concern

    You can report anonymously.

    If you're worried about something happening in a chapter, tell us. Every report goes to our Vice President of Judicial Affairs, who reviews it under our judicial process.

    You can also call the national Anti-Hazing Hotline at 1-888-NOT-HAZE (1-888-668-4293). Calls are anonymous.

    If someone is in danger, call 911 first.
